# BALANCE HisA/TrpF cross-domain boundary audit v1

## Question

Can experimental enzyme evolution provide a non-floral system in which a multifunctional shared solution persists or competes with a differentiated/duplicated solution, making it a candidate for BALANCE's sandwiched-world logic?

## Evidence chain

### 2012 IAD experiment

Näsvall et al. 2012 (Science, DOI `10.1126/science.1226521`) started from a bifunctional HisA-derived allele in `Salmonella enterica` that performed both HisA and weak TrpF activity. Under continuous selection for both functions, amplification and divergence generated specialist and generalist enzyme trajectories and faster growth.

This establishes that a shared multifunctional molecular state and differentiated copies can both occur within one experimentally evolved system.

### Duplication cost

Adler et al. 2014 (Molecular Biology and Evolution, DOI `10.1093/molbev/msu111`) experimentally measured substantial costs and instability of gene amplification/duplication in bacterial systems. In their defined 8-kbp amplification system, each extra copy cost about 1.2% relative fitness, corresponding to roughly 0.15% per additional kbp; amplifications also segregated rapidly when selection was relaxed.

This is not a measurement of `K` in the 2012 HisA/TrpF evolution experiment, but it demonstrates that duplication-like architecture costs can be biologically large enough to oppose specialization.

### 2026 bifunctional rescue

Näsvall & Abdalaal 2026 (G3, DOI `10.1093/g3journal/jkag167`) evolved `Delta trpF` Salmonella populations from wild-type relevant genes. Bifunctional rescue arose through mutations in `hisA` or `trpA` while retaining ancestral function. Target-gene duplication occurred in only one population and did not show functional divergence.

This provides strong boundary evidence that adaptive resolution can remain in a multifunctional/generalist state rather than obligatorily entering a duplicated-specialist state.

## BALANCE interpretation

The system is a high-value **cross-domain architecture-outcome candidate** because it contains the ingredients BALANCE predicts should matter:

```text
real multifunctional demand
shared/generalist solutions
differentiated/duplicated solutions
demonstrated biological cost/instability of duplication in related bacterial systems
alternative evolutionary outcomes across experiments.
```

It therefore motivates the possibility of a molecular BALANCE domain in which conflict exists but duplication/specialization does not yet outrank the shared multifunctional solution.

## Why this is not yet a direct BALANCE receipt

No cited experiment currently provides a matched, same-context direct comparison of:

```text
W_S* = optimized shared/bifunctional architecture fitness
W_D* = optimized duplicated/differentiated architecture fitness
```

while holding environmental conditions, genetic background, opportunity set and fitness scale fixed in the way required by BALANCE.

The 2014 duplication-cost measurements are from defined amplification systems rather than an independently measured `K` for the 2012 HisA/TrpF lineages. The 2012 and 2026 experiments also differ in starting genotype and selection design.

Therefore current status is:

```text
G3_CROSS_DOMAIN_ARCHITECTURE_BOUNDARY_CANDIDATE
DIRECT_BALANCE_WORLDLINE_NOT_IDENTIFIED
```

## Stronger future test

A BALANCE-grade microbial design would construct, in one background and environment:

1. an optimized bifunctional single-copy/generalist genotype;
2. a matched duplicated or functionally separated genotype;
3. direct competition or growth-fitness estimates on the same scale;
4. controlled variation in demand intensity and duplication/deployment cost.

Then test whether a finite region exists where dual-function conflict is active but the shared/bifunctional genotype retains higher optimized fitness.

## Claim ceiling

Appropriate:

> HisA/TrpF evolution provides an experimental molecular candidate for testing whether shared multifunctionality can remain favored when specialization is possible but duplication carries costs.

Not appropriate:

> Existing HisA/TrpF experiments already identify a BALANCE middle-world interval or estimate its direct `W_S* - W_D*` reserve.
