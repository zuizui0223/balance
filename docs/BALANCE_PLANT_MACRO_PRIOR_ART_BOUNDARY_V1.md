# BALANCE plant macro prior-art boundary v1

## Purpose

Prevent the plant macro programme from claiming as new what is already established within its three major literature families.

## U1 — herbivory and pollination

Haas & Lortie (2020) already provide a systematic review showing that herbivory can alter floral traits, pollinator visitation and reproductive outcomes across a broad literature.

BALANCE therefore does not claim novelty for the statement that herbivores can affect pollination or reproduction.

Its distinct target is the **architecture of conflict resolution** once the same reproductive system is exposed to multiple functional demands.

## U2 — sexual interference

Barrett (2002) and the sexual-interference literature already develop the idea that male and female function can interfere and that dichogamy, herkogamy, movement of sexual organs and floral polymorphisms may reduce interference.

BALANCE therefore does not claim novelty for 'sexual interference exists' or 'herkogamy/dichogamy can reduce interference'.

The independent comparative question is whether the *spatiotemporal geometry of conflict* predicts which architecture mode is observed across conflict families under one codebook.

## U3 — heteranthery

Vallejo-Marín et al. (2010) already establish that heteranthery occurs in 16 analysed families across 12 orders and is phylogenetically associated with poricidal anthers, absence/loss of nectaries and enantiostyly.

The same paper explicitly raises developmental/genetic constraints as a possible reason heteranthery is rare, and its division-of-labour discussion frames heteranthery as a response to pollen being both pollinator food and male gamete.

Vallejo-Marín et al. (2009) experimentally show in *Solanum rostratum* that feeding anthers are preferentially manipulated while pollinating anthers export more pollen.

BALANCE therefore must not claim as its novelty:

- that pollen feeding creates a potential conflict;
- that heteranthery can divide feeding and pollinating roles;
- that heteranthery correlates with poricidal anthers, nectar state or enantiostyly;
- that developmental constraints could limit heteranthery.

## BALANCE-owned comparative question

The independent macro claim to test is broader:

> Across distinct reproductive functional conflicts, do pre-existing module structure and the timing/space of competing demands predict whether the system remains integrated, separates tasks temporally or spatially, or divides labour structurally?

This claim requires one common coding system spanning at least the U1 and U2 outcome-blind universes, with U3 contributing structural-positive anchors only under an explicitly matched design.

## What would count as a genuinely new result

A publishable positive result would be something like:

- module substrate predicts structural module division after conflict family and phylogenetic dependence are controlled;
- sequential conflict predicts temporal separation while simultaneous conflict on repeated modules predicts structural partition;
- environmental/population mosaics predict context-dependent architecture rather than one fixed solution;
- the same architecture rule recurs across pollinator–antagonist, sexual-interference, pollen-fate and pollinator–prey conflict families.

These are cross-conflict architecture results, not restatements of any one source literature.

## Negative-result value

A null cross-family result is also informative if the codebook is reliable: it would show that conflict-resolution mode is family/mechanism-specific rather than governed by a simple modularity or spatiotemporal rule.

## SLK boundary

None of these comparative associations identify `R`, `K`, `Phi`, accessibility, invasion, fixation or occupancy in the SLK sense.

BALANCE macro remains an empirical comparative programme about observed conflict/architecture states and their ecological correlates.
