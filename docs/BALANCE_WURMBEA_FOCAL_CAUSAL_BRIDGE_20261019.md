# Wurmbea: biological conflict-to-sex-architecture causal handoff (2026-10-19)

**Status: external primary-source-supported validation candidate arising from an independently acquired multi-species public trait screen; not a new field experiment, primary raw-data replay or proof of historical conflict-driven trait architecture formation.**

## Why Wurmbea, rather than another arbitrary famous specialization case?

The independently source-acquired [AusTraits v7 congeneric frontier](BALANCE_AUSTRAITS_V7_STRUCTURE_LEVEL_AND_LINEAGE_V1.md) identifies **14 strict hermaphroditic and 3 sex-separated Wurmbea species**, with **eight** species also having exact `insect` pollen-syndrome records (five shared-sex and three sex-separated). All are identified using one raw-label state per exact binomial, but neither organismal phylogeny nor WFO accepted-name authorship has been independently reconciled. This is a candidate-genus result, not an independently reconstructed evolutionary transition.

Crucially, separate **original Wurmbea studies** directly investigated reproductive conflict, relative sex-morph fitness, experimentally manipulated ecology, and sexual-system phylogenetics. Their repeated study systems are **not independently sampled species**, and their fitness outcomes do not all share one scale.

## Exact sources and what each legitimately contributes

### W1: Within-species geographical mosaic (Barrett 1992)

- Source: **Barrett 1992**, *Journal of Evolutionary Biology* 5:423–444, DOI [10.1046/j.1420-9101.1992.5030423.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1420-9101.1992.5030423.x).
- Surveyed **45 W. dioica populations** across regions of Australia. Sexual-system variation includes gender-monomorphic and gender-dimorphic populations; monomorphic populations were often observed on richer/moister sites, while dimorphic populations were more frequent on dry/shallow-soil sites. Flowering times and environments differed.
- **Admitted:** real population-scale variation within a named taxon under different ecological environments. **Not admitted:** manipulation of soil conditions, direct causal origin or a unique ancestral direction.

### W2: Inbreeding cost and morph fitness in W. biglandulosa (Ramsey, Vaughton & Peakall 2006)

- Source: *Evolution* 60:529–537, DOI [10.1111/j.0014-3820.2006.tb01134.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0014-3820.2006.tb01134.x). The legacy publisher DOI **10.1554/04-433.1** points to this **same article**, not a second replication.
- Experimentally selfed versus crossed flowers; published seed-set inbreeding depression **delta=0.86**. The original study also tested intact versus emasculated sex morphs in the field and reported very strong cumulative inbreeding depression approaching **0.99**.
- **Admitted:** a quantitative, context-relevant causal biological route by which selfing and pollen transfer can change the female fitness benefit of sex segregation. **Not admitted:** measured complete male + female lifetime fitness or the costed origin of a new module.

### W3: Direct ecological manipulation of relative female advantage in W. dioica (Ramsey, Vaughton & Peakall 2006)

- Source: *Journal of Evolutionary Biology* 19:1497–1506, DOI [10.1111/j.1420-9101.2006.01129.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2006.01129.x).
- In **two subdioecious populations**, assessed seed production, outcrossing and inbreeding depression. Fruiting male plants produced fewer than half the seeds of females; inbreeding depression estimates exceeded 0.85.
- In its **glasshouse ecological regime comparison**, the reported female fitness advantage relative to fruiting males was **3.7 under open pollination**, shrinking to **1.4 when the fruiting males' outcrossing rate was experimentally increased**.
- **Admitted:** reproductive-fitness advantage of a sexual morph changes strongly with mating context. This is closer to a causal change in an architecture's *component payoff* than any present-day trait–pollination association in the broad AusTraits source.
- **Not admitted:** a controlled generation of new dioecy, random assignment of genotype/architecture, a net architecture `Phi=R-K` on combined sex fitness, or historical state transition.

### W4: Manipulated resource/sex plasticity (Vaughton & Ramsey 2011, issue 2012)

- Source: *Annals of Botany* 109:521–530, DOI [10.1093/aob/mcr163](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcr163), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278287/).
- Compared cosexual and gynodioecious populations of *W. biglandulosa* with subdioecious and dioecious populations of *W. dioica*. Manipulated **high/low resource regimes** and recorded flowers bearing female function, male pollen performance, and sex-dependent plasticity.
- For phenotypic males in the subdioecious *W. dioica* population, reported gender plasticity **66%** under high versus **42%** under low resources. Plasticity costs were reported only for the subdioecious *W. dioica* context; other biological contexts did not uniformly show such costs.
- **Admitted:** ecology can alter male/female function allocation and phenotypic sex expression while sex architecture itself remains a distinct trait state.
- **Not admitted:** pure-function `z_{F1}^*` and `z_{F2}^*` or independent structural costs, and certainly not experimentally generated historic sex-architecture evolution.

### W5: Molecular history with genuine reconstruction ambiguity (Case et al. 2008)

- Source: *International Journal of Plant Sciences* 169:141–156, DOI [10.1086/523368](https://doi.org/10.1086/523368).
- Four noncontiguous plastid regions (about **5.5 kb**) sampled across geographically structured *Wurmbea* and outgroups; the Australian lineage exhibits a broad mixture of gender monomorphism and dimorphism. The study explicitly finds **uncertainty for ancestral sexual state assignments across nodes** and challenges the monophyly of polymorphic *W. dioica* and *W. biglandulosa*.
- **Admitted:** real phylogenetic evidence for lability and need to model ancestry, geography, species boundaries and gene-tree/species-tree disagreement.
- **Not admitted:** a reliable number or direction of independent historic transitions, or evidence that a quantified particular functional conflict preceded each new sexual architecture.

## Strongest next falsifiable within-system causal question

**When does selfing and resource-mediated male/female allocation conflict make a permanently sex-separated architecture fitter than a plastic shared sexual system — and does that advantage predict historical transitions once feasibility and ancestry are considered?**

- **Minimal ecological response:** joint mature-seed viable offspring and male siring on the same expected-fitness scale under crossed reproductive morph × pollination/outcross manipulation × resources; complete plant and population accounting.
- **Architecture alternatives:** separate temporary phenotypic sex plasticity, flower-level partitioning, permanent sex segregation among individuals and developmental maintenance costs rather than relabel all as 'modular'.
- **Negative control:** observed sex-morph differences within comparably insect-pollinated populations/species and ecological contexts with low selfing/inbreeding depression. A simple insect-vs-wind binary predictor is not the direct ecological pressure in this candidate.
- **Evolutionary accessibility and history:** classify heritable modules and infer population-resolved histories on credible organellar and nuclear trees with monophyly and introgression/coalescence uncertainty; test alternative scenarios with sex-specific selection, drift and pre-existing exaptation. W5's unresolved ancestral-state assignments must remain unresolved rather than converted into a chosen direction.
