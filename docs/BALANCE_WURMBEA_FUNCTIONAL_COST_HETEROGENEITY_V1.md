# Wurmbea plasticity and opposing male/female allocation: cross-sexual-system primary-source receipt

**Date:** 2026-10-20. **Status:** newly organized original-published comparative observation (not newly field-measured data or an independent source-derived taxonomic sample). Purpose: distinguish measured sex-function trade-off proxies from mere simultaneous multifunctionality and expose alternative systems where plasticity remains viable.

**One original controlled-resource source**: Vaughton & Ramsey, *Gender plasticity and sexual system stability in Wurmbea*, Annals of Botany 109:521–530 (online 2011; volume 2012), DOI [10.1093/aob/mcr163](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcr163); [public full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278287/). Several population/sex-morph comparisons below originate in **this single study**, so they are not independent macroevolutionary replications.

## A. Published results by population / sexual system

| Focal population strategy | Plasticity in phenotypic males under high/low resources | Male:hermaphrodite pollen per flower in source comparison | Proxy cost of increased female flower expression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *W. biglandulosa*, cosexual (hermaphrodites common) | **100%** expressed perfect flowers in both treatment regimes | **~0.6** (males ~40% fewer grains than hermaphrodites) | Pollen per flower: **no significant relationship**; flower diameter: **positive** correlation with number of perfect flowers |
| *W. biglandulosa*, gynodioecious (females + hermaphrodites) | **100%** in both regimes | **~0.6** | Pollen per flower: **positive**; flower diameter: **positive** |
| *W. dioica*, subdioecious (females, males, hermaphrodites) | **66% high resources; 42% low resources** | **~2.0** (males twice hermaphrodite pollen per flower; F(1,38)=9.70, P=0.003) | Pollen per flower: **negative**; flower diameter: **negative** |
| *W. dioica*, putatively dioecious (males + females; no observed hermaphrodites) | **0%** expressed perfect flowers in either regime | **not comparable** (no hermaphrodites to define denominator) | **not assayed** in hermaphrodites, which were absent |

These are source-reported per-flower pollen production, not measured *male siring fitness*, seed fitness or common-scale lifetime fitness. Pollen/diameter-versus-perfect-flower associations are **indirect plasticity-cost proxies** from multiple regression controlling **total flower number**; they are not randomized interventions on floral sex-state gene architecture or measurements of switching cost.

The source's three populations with hermaphrodites contributed *different plant samples* for these regressions: **147** subdioecious *W. dioica*, **50** gynodioecious *W. biglandulosa*, and **79** cosexual *W. biglandulosa*. These are within-study sample sizes, not distinct lineage transitions. Sex-morph comparisons were also conditional on plant size/resource environment. Male and hermaphrodite pollen output in the two *W. biglandulosa* populations is confounded with male plants being smaller; do not interpret 0.6 as a genetically caused cost of fixed male specialization.

## B. Strong ecological interpretation and its boundary

**Qualitatively opposite male-output advantage is actually observed across two sexual-system contexts.** In subdioecious *W. dioica*, phenotypic males have *higher* pollen-per-flower output than hermaphrodites; in cosexual/gynodioecious *W. biglandulosa*, males have *lower* pollen-per-flower output. Meanwhile, the proxy for cost of concurrently expressing female function is negative only in the subdioecious context and absent/positive in the *W. biglandulosa* contexts.

Therefore a universal binary assertion `separate sex architecture > reversible plasticity` is false as an interpretation of these empirical pollen and response data: **the payoff to maintaining/partitioning sex functions is highly ecological- and population-context-dependent**. This is a biologically motivated comparison and **does not identify actual lifetime reproductive fitness of alternative heritable sexual architectures**.

This maps to the cross-repository causal programme as follows:

- **SCH:** opposing function gradients must be verified on the *same manipulated coordinate*; the reported partial negative correlations are candidates, not a source-certified causal conflict load `L`.
- **BALANCE:** co-occurring male/female function may be maintained if its ecological cost is low or non-detected in a given source context; the cosexual/gynodioecious results alone do not estimate `W_S^* - W_D^*`.
- **PAYOFF and SLK:** in a properly declared dynamical comparison, the competitive alternatives include shared compromise, a cue-reversible phenotype, and a heritable separated architecture. These empirical response proportions do **not** supply cue accuracy, environmental turnover, instantaneous switching cost, module maintenance cost, or genotype invasion conditions.
- **BITA:** separate ecological routes (pollen siring, seed production, pollination access, inbreeding) are not a uniquely identified channel decomposition from a pollen-output effect alone.

**Critical negative control:** *W. dioica* dioecy is currently classified by stable sex expression in the sampled population. Its zero observed perfect-flower response is **partly definition-/sampling-linked**, not independent molecular evidence of permanently genetically canalized sex determination. Nuclear developmental genetics remain required.

## C. What this source can test in an expanded macro design

Pre-register an **independent taxon sampling frame** before coding sexually separated outcome. Across multiple related genera/sister lineages, collect matching assays of:

1. Female-function investment and pollen siring measured together under common-resource manipulations;
2. Reversibility of sex expression within identified/marked individuals;
3. Concurrent temporal overlap of sex functions within floral structures;
4. Longitudinal population histories, accepted-name identity, nuclear sex genotype and selection on competing morphs.

Wurmbea supplies a useful **focal ecology/provenance example**; the comparisons above are a single study with population/lineage confounding and cannot be meta-analyzed as four independent data sets.

## D. Relationship to new mathematical result

The [PAYOFF conditional toy result](https://github.com/zuizui0223/payoff/blob/theory/plastic-compromise-to-modules-20261020/theory/PLASTIC_COMPROMISE_VERSUS_FIXED_ARCHITECTURE.md) predicts two independent ways modules can become favorable: (i) genuine *simultaneous* function conflict persists despite perfect cue-driven adjustment, and (ii) expensive/inaccurate phenotype switching under ecological turnover makes fixed architecture relatively attractive. **Neither ecological mechanism is quantitatively estimated** by the present Wurmbea source data. The reported pollen associations are **closer to an ecological conflict candidate** than a direct `C` or `K` receipt.

**Claim ceiling:** we have an original, explicitly single-study observation of sex-function payoff heterogeneity consistent with conditional costs/benefits of plastic expression. No nuclear sexual-transition history, independently matched alternative architecture total fitness, or trait-conflict-caused origin has been identified.
