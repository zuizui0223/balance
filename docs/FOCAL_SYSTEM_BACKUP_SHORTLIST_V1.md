# BALANCE–BITA–SLK focal-system backup shortlist v1

## Decision rule

The flagship system is chosen by identification closure, not by natural-history appeal.

Required minimum:

```text
F1  causal manipulation of focal A
F2  causal manipulation of focal D
F3  selective antagonist intervention E_G
F4  selective pollinator intervention E_P
F5  common reproductive fitness currency
F6  feasible A x D x E_G x E_P crossing
F7  independent remaining-channel assay
```

A system that fails one gate may remain useful for SCH or BALANCE but is not promoted to full BITA mechanism allocation.

## Why Pedicularis remains first

Pedicularis has unusually coherent trait semantics for the intended question:

```text
A = realized corolla exsertion above the cupulate bract
D = water-mediated protection by the cupulate bract
```

Published work supports opposing pollinator/seed-predator consequences for A and an experimental defence role for D. The weakness is technical identification: a causal A manipulation and a truly selective E_G intervention are not yet demonstrated.

Therefore Pedicularis remains first only under a fail-closed pilot. Failure of E_G selectivity is a system-level rejection criterion for full BITA, not a reason to weaken the mechanism-allocation standard.

## Why Fragaria is the first fallback

Published `Fragaria vesca` work already crossed herbivory presence/absence with pollination treatment in a full-factorial common garden. This makes the consumer-intervention layer unusually mature.

However the published experiment estimated selection on measured attraction- and defence-related traits; it did not independently manipulate one registered attraction trait A and one registered defence trait D in the BITA sense.

The next qualification question for Fragaria is therefore:

```text
Can one attraction-facing trait and one defence-facing trait be manipulated independently,
while retaining the existing herbivory x pollination intervention architecture?
```

Only a positive answer moves Fragaria above Pedicularis.

## Why Dalechampia is not promoted now

`Dalechampia scandens` provides a strong natural conflict comparator: larger showy bracts can increase bee visitation/pollen receipt and seed-predator oviposition, and resin reward has been experimentally manipulated in other work.

But the current evidence does not supply the registered attraction-by-defence two-trait architecture plus selective consumer crossing required for the flagship. It remains useful for route recurrence and experimental-method inspiration rather than immediate execution.

## Promotion order

```text
1. Test Pedicularis Q1/Q3 feasibility.
2. In parallel, search Fragaria for independently manipulable A and D candidates compatible with its existing consumer factorial.
3. Promote Fragaria only if both trait manipulations can be made causal and orthogonal.
4. Do not broaden to additional systems until both Pedicularis and Fragaria fail the registered gates.
```

This prevents candidate hunting from replacing identification discipline.
