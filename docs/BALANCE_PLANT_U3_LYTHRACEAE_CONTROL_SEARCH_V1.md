# BALANCE U3 Lythraceae prospective control search v1

## Frozen case

The active case is `Lagerstroemia indica`.

## Close-relative search

Modern plastome phylogenies place `L. guilinensis`, `L. excelsa`, and other Clade-IV taxa near `L. indica`; Clade III is sister to the indica-containing Clade IV. The audited close candidates `L. guilinensis`, `L. excelsa`, `L. glabra`, and `L. subcostata` all retain dimorphic fertile stamens and therefore fail the frozen heteranthery-absence gate.

## Monomorphic candidates

Three biologically relevant monomorphic-stamen candidates remain.

### Lagerstroemia speciosa

Kim, Graham & Graham (1994) identify `L. speciosa` as one of two monomorphic-stamen / monomorphic-pollen taxa in their 39-species survey. Modern floras likewise describe its stamens as all about the same length. Species-level field work identifies large bees as effective pollinators and reports substantial open-pollinated fruit set.

### Lagerstroemia macrocarpa

Kim et al. (1994) identify this as the other monomorphic-stamen / monomorphic-pollen taxon in their survey, and current flora treatment describes all stamens as about the same length. A 2022 plastome analysis places it in a clade with `L. speciosa` and `L. venusta`, separate from the `L. indica` lineage. Species-level effective-pollination evidence remains open.

### Lagerstroemia parviflora

Regional flora explicitly describes 40–60 monomorphic stamens. `Apis cerana` honey analyses identify it as a major nectar source, and a later field inventory reports frequent `Apis cerana` and `A. mellifera` nectar/pollen foraging.

Those records establish bee resource use, but the current contract does not equate forage use with a source-secure effective-pollination experiment.

More importantly, `L. parviflora` is absent from the common modern Lagerstroemia plastome surfaces used to rank the other candidates. Traditional sectional classification cannot substitute for a common species-level phylogenetic ranking because modern phylogenomics explicitly shows substantial disagreement with the morphological sections.

## Why L. speciosa is not promoted

`L. speciosa` has the strongest combined morphology and pollination receipt. But source quality is a tie-break only after phylogenetic grain is fixed.

A potentially eligible monomorphic congener, `L. parviflora`, remains unranked relative to the case. Selecting the better-documented but demonstrably distant `L. speciosa` would therefore repeat exactly the convenience-control failure that the prospective design is meant to prevent.
