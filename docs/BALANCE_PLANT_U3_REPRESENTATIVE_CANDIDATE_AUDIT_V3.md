## Malvaceae correction

`Sparrmannia africana` is now an explicit **rejection**, not an open candidate.

Vogel's pollen-flower literature and later staminode work explain the conspicuous yellow outer structures as pollen-mimicking staminodes / sterile outer structures. The transferable pollen is carried by the inner fertile stamens. That is a deceptive pollen signal, but it does not establish the strict two-fertile-pollen-fate heteranthery state needed for independent U3 representative resolution.

The strongest lineage clue is now `Pseudocorchorus`.

Pre-2010 morphology places it securely in the former Tiliaceae / current Malvaceae-Grewioideae and describes:

- somewhat zygomorphic flowers;
- nearly all stamens inserted unilaterally;
- elongated subbasifixed anthers opening by pores or pores-plus-slits.

A later floral-symmetry synthesis explicitly discusses `Pseudocorchorus` in the buzz-pollination / heteranthery transition context. These properties fit the 2010 review's Malvaceae profile much better than `Sparrmannia`.

But six `Pseudocorchorus` species were recognized, and the available sources do not yet identify which single species supplied the review's unique Malvaceae report or demonstrate two contrasting functional stamen types at species level.

So the family is **not closed**.

## Bixaceae

`Amoreuxia palmatifida` remains the strongest species-level candidate. Pre-2010 sources support two stamen sets differentiated by filament length and anther colour plus poricidal anthers.

`Cochlospermum vitifolium` remains a weaker alternative because strong pollination data coexist with conflicting descriptions of whether its stamens are equal or unequal.

The review does not state that Bixaceae contributes only one heterantherous species, so even a real heterantherous species cannot be silently equated with the Table S1 representative.

## Scrophulariaceae

`Diascia anastrepta` remains the strongest species-level candidate.

Manning & Brothers (1986) explicitly describe marked heteranthery: a reduced anticous anther pair with little or no yellow pollen and a posticous pair associated with the stigma producing copious grey-green pollen.

Historical broad-Scrophulariaceae candidates `Agalinis` and `Pedicularis` remain rejected under APG III because they belong to Orobanchaceae; `Torenia` is rejected as Linderniaceae plus post-2010 evidence.

## Selection rule

A canonical representative requires either:

1. exact Supporting Table S1 linkage; or
2. secure APG-III family placement + species-level pre-2010 heteranthery evidence + an explicit review statement that the family has only one reported heterantherous species.

Current representative reconstruction therefore remains 13/16 rather than manufacturing a clean-looking 16/16 table.
