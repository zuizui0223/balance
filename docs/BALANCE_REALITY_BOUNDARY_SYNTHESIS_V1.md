# BALANCE reality-boundary synthesis v1

## Purpose

This synthesis integrates the empirical layers without mixing incompatible estimands. The goal is not to inflate the strict Q1B positive numerator. It is to identify where BALANCE-compatible conflict appears, where it does not, and where apparent opposition fails biological attribution.

## Layer A: strict positive recurrence

Three independent biological clusters meet the frozen effect-size-ready Q1B contract: Fragaria vesca, Impatiens capensis, and Gymnadenia conopsea (2015). They share a continuous focal coordinate, pollinator × antagonist manipulation or equivalent identified factorial contrast, a common downstream reproductive-fitness interpretation, and identified joint uncertainty.

The first allowed componentwise random-effects pool is therefore retained at k=3. Its point estimates are positive for both pollinator contrasts and negative for both antagonist contrasts, but all modified Knapp-Hartung 95% intervals cross zero. Two components show moderate heterogeneity. The supported claim is replicated directionally compatible conflict with context dependence, not a universal non-zero mean effect.

## Layer B: design-matched negative controls

Trifolium repens and Lythrum salicaria show that factorial exposure to pollination and antagonistic or damage treatments does not inevitably generate Q1B-type same-coordinate conflict. Lythrum is quantitatively effect-ready as a negative control; Trifolium has public raw data and reproducible models but lacks the required same-trait opposition.

These negatives are part of the inference, not discarded nulls. They bound the theory against an inevitable-conflict interpretation.

## Layer C: boundary estimands

Ipomopsis provides a sequential-filter boundary: strong pollination-stage selection can coexist with weak or undetected seed-predation selection on the same trait. Primula farinosa provides strict opposing selection on a discrete genetic morph, but this estimand is intentionally not converted into a continuous standardized beta.

These systems show that conflict can be real while falling outside the strict Q1B factorial vector.

## Layer D: attribution failure

Chapurlat et al. Gymnadenia (2019) shows that pollinator and residual non-pollinator gradients can oppose one another even when an antagonist mechanism is not supported. Therefore statistical opposition alone is insufficient for biological-mechanism attribution.

## Boundary interpretation

Together, the empirical synthesis supports a bounded statement:

1. BALANCE-compatible opposing selection on a shared coordinate recurs in multiple independent systems.
2. It is not inevitable under superficially similar factorial designs.
3. Conflict may also appear through sequential or discrete-morph estimands that are adjacent to, but not exchangeable with, strict Q1B.
4. Statistical opposition without identified antagonist biology is not promoted as mechanistic support.

This structure is more informative than a single pooled mean because it maps the empirical support domain and its failure boundaries.

## Claim ceiling

None of these layers directly identifies optimized shared-versus-differentiated worldlines or the BALANCE state variable itself. They do not identify W_S*, W_D*, rho, Phi, xi, or d_B. Direct BALANCE occupancy remains a separate final validation problem after literature synthesis.
