# BALANCE U3 Brassicaceae prospective control search v1

## Frozen case

The active prospective case is `Brassica rapa`.

The search is performed before any prospective control conflict or routing outcome is extracted.

## Close-relative search

The close `B. rapa` / `B. oleracea` lineage and allied Brassiceae do not provide a valid negative-architecture control.

- `Brassica oleracea`: four long + two short stamens.
- `Brassica napus`: four long + two short stamens.
- `Diplotaxis erucoides`: a close apparent relative of the rapa/oleracea lineage, but four long + two short stamens.
- `Raphanus raphanistrum`: close Brassiceae relative with excellent direct pollinator-effectiveness data, but explicitly tetradynamous.

Thus strong ecological evidence cannot rescue the morphology gate.

## Family-level widening

The search then reaches Brassicaceae taxa in which tetradynamy has reverted toward equal-height stamens.

### Stanleya elata

Nikolov (2019) directly describes all six stamens as approximately equal height. Cullen & Ashman (2024) sampled five natural `S. elata` populations and report Stanleyoid flowers as generalist insect-pollinated, with visits from large-bodied bees and wasps.

### Stanleya pinnata

A broad Brassicaceae phylogeny places sampled `S. pinnata` in the New World Thelypodieae assemblage whose members share stamens of nearly equal length. Cullen & Ashman (2024) sampled six `S. pinnata` populations and report the same generalist insect-pollination opportunity.

Both therefore satisfy the biological negative-control opportunity much better than the rejected close Brassiceae candidates.

## Why no control is selected

The matching protocol requires the closest eligible relative, not merely a convenient family-level species with excellent documentation.

Equal/nearly equal stamens occur in multiple New World Brassicaceae lineages. The current public evidence does not provide a frozen common species-level phylogenetic surface that ranks all biologically eligible equal-stamen candidates relative to `B. rapa`.

Therefore source quality cannot be used to jump directly to `S. elata` or `S. pinnata`.

The current blocker is:

At the registered public-retrieval ceiling, Brassicaceae is retained as a missing dependence block rather than assigned a convenient distant control.

## Queue consequence

The prospective routing queue may advance to `Lagerstroemia indica` only because the Brassicaceae matching-stage evidence ceiling is recorded before any control conflict/routing outcome is extracted.

## Claim ceiling

This audit establishes close-relative exclusion and the existence of biologically eligible family-level negative candidates. It does not select a Brassicaceae control and does not infer conflict or routing architecture.
