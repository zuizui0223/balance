# BALANCE manuscript Results text v1

## Conflict-compatible selection recurs, but is not inevitable

The strict quantitative synthesis recovered three independent biological systems in which pollinator and antagonist environments were factorially resolved on a shared trait coordinate with effect-size-ready joint uncertainty: *Fragaria vesca*, *Impatiens capensis*, and *Gymnadenia conopsea*. These systems define the first admissible Q1B pool. Across the four preregistered context-specific contrasts, the pooled point estimates were directionally consistent with positive pollinator-mediated and negative antagonist-mediated contributions, but uncertainty remained wide at `k = 3`. The random-effects estimate for pollinator-mediated selection when the antagonist was present was 0.143 (modified Knapp–Hartung 95% CI: -0.168 to 0.454; I2 = 0%). When the antagonist was absent, the corresponding estimate was 0.186 (-0.640 to 1.012; I2 = 62.7%). Antagonist-mediated selection under open pollination was -0.164 (-0.791 to 0.464; I2 = 65.7%), and under supplemented pollination was -0.085 (-0.331 to 0.160; I2 = 0%). Thus, all four pooled confidence intervals included zero, while two contrasts showed substantial between-system heterogeneity. At this stage the quantitative result supports replicated, directionally compatible conflict with context dependence rather than a universal non-zero mean effect.

Design-matched negative controls showed that this conflict is not an automatic consequence of factorial pollination-by-antagonist designs. In *Trifolium repens*, pollination did not independently generate the same-trait opposition required for Q1B promotion. In *Lythrum salicaria*, source-reported pollinator-mediated changes in directional selection were close to zero for inflorescence number, height, and flowering start (`-0.01 ± 0.05`, `0.00 ± 0.06`, and `-0.07 ± 0.06`, respectively), and the corresponding pollination-by-damage-by-trait contrasts were also imprecise (`-0.12 ± 0.12`, `0.11 ± 0.13`, and `0.01 ± 0.12`). These negative controls constrain the domain of the mechanism: comparable experimental designs can fail to generate detectable same-coordinate conflict.

## Related conflict structures delimit the Q1B estimand

Several biologically informative systems fell outside the strict Q1B estimand and were retained as explicit boundary cases rather than pooled with the positives. In *Ipomopsis*, strong pollination-stage selection can occur without detectable seed-predation selection on the same floral trait, producing a sequential-filter pattern rather than a factorial pollinator-by-antagonist contrast vector. In *Primula farinosa*, pollinators and grazers favour alternative genetically based scape morphs, providing a strong conflict pattern on a discrete-morph relative-fitness estimand rather than on a continuous standardized selection gradient. A second *Gymnadenia* study recovered opposing pollinator and residual nonpollinator selection on scent compounds, but the residual component was not biologically identified as an antagonist. This attribution failure shows that numerical opposition alone is insufficient for antagonist-specific support.

Together, the positive, negative, and boundary cases sharpen the empirical definition of the mechanism. Q1B requires more than two coefficients of opposite sign: the biological agents, shared coordinate, common reproductive-fitness interpretation, estimand structure, and joint uncertainty must all be compatible. Keeping these cases separate prevents an apparent increase in replication from being produced by estimand drift.

## Persistence-geometry signatures recur across a broader literature

The source-adjudicated BALANCE pattern ledger contained 17 independent biological clusters spanning plant, protein, gene-regulatory, genome-architecture, and vertebrate-morphology systems. Nine clusters expressed middle-regime signatures. Seven were classified as conflict without splitting, one as persistent integration with an accessible alternative, and one as a sandwiched transition mosaic. The same ledger also contained two boundary-crossing clusters, one direct-differentiation boundary, and five unresolved clusters. The 17 clusters therefore do not estimate the natural prevalence of BALANCE. Instead, they show that the qualitative geometry required by the theory—active conflict combined with persistence of a shared or intermediate state—recurs across independent systems and domains.

The quantitative Q1B synthesis and the broader pattern ledger occupy different inferential layers. Q1B provides a narrowly defined quantitative test that opposing agent-mediated selection can recur on one shared coordinate, together with matched negative controls showing that such conflict is not inevitable. The broader ledger asks whether persistence and transition geometries compatible with the sandwiched regime recur beyond those few systems for which a common quantitative estimand can be reconstructed. The agreement of these layers supports recurrence of the theory's prerequisite and qualitative target pattern without treating either layer as direct measurement of BALANCE occupancy.

## Direct BALANCE occupancy remains unidentified

The literature synthesis did not recover a direct matched comparison between an optimized shared architecture and a registered accessible differentiated alternative measured on the same fitness scale, in the same context, with joint uncertainty. The direct-worldline reserve therefore remains empty. Consequently, the current empirical synthesis does not identify `W_S*`, `W_D*`, `rho = W_S* - W_D*`, `Phi`, `xi`, or `d_B`, and it does not establish that any focal natural system occupies the BALANCE region in the strict worldline sense.

This gap defines rather than weakens the final empirical target. The literature synthesis establishes that conflict-compatible shared-coordinate patterns recur, that such conflict is not inevitable, that related conflict structures occupy identifiable boundary classes, and that middle-regime persistence signatures recur across a broader set of systems. What remains is a final-stage matched worldline test that directly compares the shared and differentiated alternatives under a common fitness definition.
