# BALANCE math -> direct/causal signatures v1

## Purpose

Translate the current middle-world mathematics into prospectively testable Chapter-2 signatures. BALANCE is validated by direct same-context worldline comparisons and environmental/domain geometry, not by PAYOFF invasion outcomes.

## Signature B1 — direct sandwiched state

Primary state condition:

\[
L>0,
\qquad
W_S^*>\max_jW_{D_j}^*.
\]

**Test:** establish positive same-context SCH conflict and directly optimize/estimate the shared world against every registered accessible alternative architecture on one fitness scale.

**Failure:** conflict is absent, or any definitely accessible alternative matches/beats the shared world.

## Signature B2 — accessibility-scope monotonicity

For nested alternative registries `A subset A'`,

\[
B_{A'}\subseteq B_A,
\qquad
\rho_{A'}\le\rho_A.
\]

**Test:** prospectively add alternative architectures in a declared audit sequence.

**Failure:** adding an alternative raises the correctly computed best-alternative reserve without changing the fitness definition/context; that would indicate an implementation or scope mismatch.

## Signature B3 — robust inner / optimistic outer domain

If

\[
A_{def}\subseteq A_{true}\subseteq A_{poss},
\]

then

\[
B_{A_{poss}}
\subseteq B_{true}
\subseteq B_{A_{def}}.
\]

**Test:** report `ROBUST_BALANCE`, `ROBUST_NON_BALANCE`, or `ACCESSIBILITY_SCOPE_UNRESOLVED` rather than forcing one state when accessibility is incompletely known.

## Signature B4 — scope fragility

Define

\[
\Gamma_A=\rho_{def}-\rho_{poss}.
\]

This is the exact width of the reserve identified set due only to accessibility uncertainty.

**Test:** report `rho_def`, `rho_poss`, `Gamma_A`, and the corresponding depth interval.

**Failure:** the claimed robust state/depth lies outside these set-theoretic bounds.

## Signature B5 — threat-switch directional derivative

At a tie among active alternative threats,

\[
D\rho_A(e;v)
=
\min_{j\in A(e)}\nabla\rho_j(e)^Tv.
\]

**Test:** around an experimentally or naturally resolved threat switch, estimate one-sided slopes for each active alternative rather than imposing one smooth derivative across the kink.

**Failure:** envelope slope lies outside the active-component directional rule under the registered smooth component worldlines.

## Signature B6 — fitness center versus environmental center

Fitness-deepest state equalizes fitness margins under monotone opposing margins:

\[
L=\rho_A.
\]

Metric-environmental center equalizes normalized distances to binding boundaries and need not occur at the same context.

**Test:** estimate both fitness margins and the preregistered environmental metric/gradients.

**Failure:** do not label a raw midpoint in environmental coordinates as the deepest BALANCE state without satisfying the relevant center definition.

## Signature B7 — sparse center certificate

In `p` environmental dimensions with affine status boundaries, an optimal basic metric-Chebyshev center has a certificate using at most `p+1` independent active constraints in `(e,t)`.

**Test:** report which biological/status or feasible-region boundaries bind the deepest context.

**Interpretation:** a study-domain boundary in the certificate means robustness is partly sampling/accessibility limited rather than purely biologically centered.

## Signature B8 — concave-domain chord certificate

If `L(e)` and every registered architecture reserve `rho_j(e)` are concave on a convex environmental domain, then the static BALANCE region is convex.

For any two BALANCE endpoints `e_0,e_1` and `e_t=(1-t)e_0+t e_1`, each margin obeys

\[
\boxed{
f(e_t)\ge(1-t)f(e_0)+t f(e_1).}
\]

Therefore two positive endpoints certify the whole straight segment as BALANCE **once concavity has been independently justified**.

**Test:** reserve endpoint contexts for the continuous-segment certificate and use held-out interior contexts for the Jensen audit

\[
r_f(t)=f(e_t)-[(1-t)f(e_0)+t f(e_1)]\ge0.
\]

Apply this separately to `L` and every registered reserve, or equivalently to the best-alternative reserve after verifying the registered concavity conditions.

**Failure:** a held-out interior margin falls below its endpoint chord beyond tolerance, or an interior context leaves BALANCE between two certified BALANCE endpoints. This rejects the declared concave static-domain model; it is not evidence for hysteresis by itself.

**Use:** this generalizes the one-dimensional affine endpoint certificate to nonlinear concave margins and multidimensional environmental chords.

## Signature B9 — strong-concavity bulge

If a registered status margin `f` has directional curvature bounded by

\[
\alpha Q\preceq-\nabla^2 f\preceq\beta Q
\]

along the environmental chord, then with `v=e_1-e_0`

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\alpha}{2}t(1-t)v^TQv
\le
f(e_t)-[(1-t)f(e_0)+tf(e_1)]
\le
\frac{\beta}{2}t(1-t)v^TQv.
}
\]

**Test:** use two endpoint contexts plus a preregistered midpoint/interior context and compare the observed bulge with the curvature interval. At the midpoint the predicted bulge is between `alpha v^TQv/8` and `beta v^TQv/8`.

**Failure:** negative bulge rejects concavity; a bulge outside the registered interval rejects the claimed curvature floor/ceiling or the common static worldline branch.

**Interpretation:** B8 says the middle world cannot secretly disappear between two BALANCE endpoints; B9 additionally predicts how much interior fitness reserve should be created by curvature.
