# BALANCE U3 Malvaceae prospective control search v1

## Frozen case

The final prospective expansion case is `Mollia lepidota`.

The control search remains predictor-blind: no prospective control conflict or routing outcome is used for candidate selection.

## Source-resolved congeners

The classical Mollia record gives four especially informative congeneric receipts.

Richard Spruce explicitly described `M. speciosa`, `M. lepidota`, `M. tomentosa`, and `M. longifolia` as having five outer longer stamen cohorts and five inner shorter cohorts, with different pollen/anther colours. An independent Flora Brasiliensis treatment states that the exterior stamens of `M. gracilis` are nearly twice as long as the interior stamens.

Therefore the source-resolved close congeners audited here all fail the frozen heteranthery-absence gate.

The 1978 generic treatment likewise characterizes the usual Mollia androecium as two whorls of five phalanges with the outer whorl longer than the inner.

## Why the congeneric search is still not formally exhausted

A 2025 description added `Mollia trimera`, distinguished by a rare trimerous external floral plan including the outer androecium. The publicly accessible description does not establish whether its fertile stamens satisfy the U3 negative-architecture criterion.

More importantly, the same modern taxonomic discussion states that Mollia monophyly has not been explicitly tested and its infrageneric relationships remain unknown.

Current taxonomy also recognizes additional congeners beyond the historically source-resolved set. The generic morphology makes continued stamen differentiation plausible, but the new divergent species demonstrates why a genus-level description cannot be used as a substitute for an exhaustive species-level absence audit.

Thus two necessary matching questions remain open together:

1. does any current Mollia congener actually pass the frozen heteranthery-absence gate?
2. if one does, is it the closest eligible congener to `M. lepidota`?

## Why the search does not jump outside Mollia

The matching hierarchy prefers a congeneric control. Widening into Grewieae before the congeneric negative-control surface and Mollia species relationships are closed would create a convenience match.

For example, `Trichospermum mexicanum` is in the broader Grewieae neighborhood but itself has unequal male-flower filaments (5–10 mm), and Grewieae relationships around Mollia remain incompletely resolved across public trees. Neither fact licenses skipping the unresolved congeneric layer.

## Programme consequence

All four prospectively frozen expansion families have now reached a terminal matching-stage state without any control-side conflict/routing outcome being used to decide progression.

The expansion queue is exhausted with four `EVIDENCE_CEILING_BLOCKED` dependence blocks. This is an informative design result: strict predictor-blind negative-control construction is substantially harder than collecting additional heteranthery-positive cases.

## Claim ceiling

This audit establishes congeneric morphology failures and the current public-evidence ceiling. It does not establish absence of a valid Mollia control in nature, does not select an outgroup control, and does not infer conflict or routing architecture.
