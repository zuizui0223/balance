# BALANCE Pedicularis geographic-conflict R-layer audit v1

## Source

Sun S-G, Armbruster WS, Huang S-Q. 2016. `Geographic consistency and variation in conflicting selection generated by pollinators and seed predators.` Annals of Botany 118:227-237. DOI `10.1093/aob/mcw097`.

## Why this enters the R layer

The study surveyed floral traits and reproductive components in 14 populations of `Pedicularis rex`; stigmatic pollen load was measured across 14 populations and seed production / pre-dispersal seed predation across 12 populations.

The focal architecture is still shared:

```text
one corolla-exsertion / floral-display coordinate
serves pollinator attraction / pollen receipt
and affects exposure to pre-dispersal seed predation
```

No second differentiated architecture is directly compared in the paper.

## Recovered conflict

The primary source reports that greater corolla exsertion beyond the cupular bracts increased stigmatic pollen load, while seed predation also increased with exsertion.

The resulting directional interpretation is explicitly opposing:

```text
pollinator-mediated selection
-> greater exsertion

seed-predator-mediated selection
-> reduced exsertion above the protective water pools
```

This is therefore admitted as:

```text
CONFLICT_WITHOUT_SPLITTING
```

The row is a single independent study/system cluster rather than fourteen independent clusters. Population replication is used as moderator information within the cluster; treating the 12-14 populations as independent literature replications would inflate the R-layer denominator.

## Geographic moderator result

The study also recovers a useful moderator asymmetry.

Pollinator-mediated selection was comparatively geographically consistent, whereas seed-predator pressure and its contribution to final viable seed production varied significantly among populations.

This provides an empirical pattern corresponding to a central BALANCE/SCH expectation:

```text
one functional demand can remain comparatively stable
while the opposing demand changes across context
-> location of the compromise / persistence boundary can move
```

The paper does not directly identify that BALANCE boundary; it supplies a geographic conflict-weight pattern that can later be tested against the Chapter-2 worldline prediction.

## Why this is not direct BALANCE occupancy

The source does not compare:

```text
W_S*  optimized shared architecture
W_D*  optimized differentiated / second-axis architecture
```

on one matched fitness scale.

It therefore does not identify `rho`, `Phi`, `xi`, `d_B`, a BALANCE width, or a direct architecture threshold.

Claim ceiling:

```text
geographic opposing selection on a persistent shared architecture
NOT direct BALANCE occupancy
```

## Quantitative-synthesis role

This study joins `Dalechampia scandens` and `Castilleja linariaefolia` as a third high-confidence candidate cluster for a floral opposing-selection quantitative stratum.

That does **not** make the stratum meta-analysis-ready.

Before pooling, recover for each system a compatible within-study selection representation. Preferred target is a bivariate standardized directional-selection vector on one biologically defined increasing trait coordinate:

```text
theta_i = (beta_pollinator_i, beta_antagonist_i)
```

with the within-study covariance matrix.

A derived opposition contrast can then be reported as:

```text
D_i = beta_pollinator_i - beta_antagonist_i
Var(D_i) = Var(beta_pollinator_i)
         + Var(beta_antagonist_i)
         - 2 Cov(beta_pollinator_i, beta_antagonist_i)
```

but `D_i` alone is not sufficient to diagnose opposing signs; the component estimates and intervals remain primary.

## Extraction limitation

The published study uses GLM / model-selection analyses and reports geographic interaction structure. It is not automatically on the same coefficient scale as the `Dalechampia` integrated-selection analysis or the `Castilleja` path decomposition.

Therefore the current R-layer ledger retains:

```text
quantitative_pool_eligible = false
```

until either:

1. compatible coefficients and their covariance are recovered from publication/supplementary material; or
2. raw/individual data permit a common reanalysis.

Missing covariance must not be replaced by zero merely to make a meta-analysis possible.

## Next use

The geographic population structure makes this study especially useful for the later moderator question:

```text
Does antagonist-pressure variation explain movement toward or away from a shared middle regime when pollinator demand remains comparatively stable?
```

That prediction remains to be tested with architecture/worldline data rather than inferred from the selection mosaic alone.
